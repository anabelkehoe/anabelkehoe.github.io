SEDS@UCSD

Riptide

Riptide was archived in 2026. I worked on the project from October 2024 to June 2026.

Riptide was a fifteen-foot, 1300lb, LOX and LNG self-landing rocket inspired by advances from SpaceX, Intuitive Machines, and Masten Space Systems, pushing the boundaries of collegiate aerospace engineering. Designed to compete in the Definity Project’s Lander Challenge, it aimed to reach 200ft in altitude and land within a 32ft diameter circle fully autonomously. Riptide had also won project of the year at SEDS SpaceVision 2023.

Tidepool

I am the lead electrical engineer for the new self-landing rocket, Tidepool, which started in June 2026.

Tidepool is the new self-landing rocket designed with the lessons learned from Riptide. It will replace Riptide to compete in the Definity Project’s Lander Challenge.

My Role: Director of Electrical Engineering

For Tidepool, I am the lead designer for the entire Electrical system, including both the power and controls systems. My focus will be on the hardware for these systems (as that is my specialty), but I will lead the software team in research and task delegation.

All of the PCBs will be designed using Altium Designer and integrated into the mechanical system using Fusion 360.

I also contributed to all system tests such as leak checks and cold flows for Riptide, and will do the same for Tidepool.

Projects and PCB Design

Smart Efuse

Designed a smart eFuse PCB in Altium Designer with programmable fault protection, incorporating delayed restart logic, dynamic fault timing, and real-time current-proportional voltage sensing to actively protect and manage power distribution in high-risk aerospace electrical systems.

Data Collection system for Riptide

This 2 board system was originally designed by an older member. I sourced the components on the board, solved all errors that occurred, created the Bill of Materials, and performed the full assembly and testing. I worked on version 2 of the board to solve issues that appeared during testing. For instance, some traces had to be cut out of the board using a knife, and some connectors were difficult to use and fragile.

This system was designed in Altium Designer, and features 3 I2C buses, each consisting of 3 ADC chips and 3 MCP chips to read from 9 sensors.

Likewise, the microcontroller’s output pins are used to control 21 relays (outputting 24V).

The boards convert from I2C –> CAN through the Teensy and are transmitted throughout the system through the CAN transceivers.

Battery Protection Circuit

Designed a battery protection and monitoring circuit that integrates both analog sensing and digital logic to enable real-time supervision of two 7-cell battery packs, including autonomous fault detection and system shutdown under unsafe conditions such as overcurrent or cell imbalance. This circuit uses 2 chips for fault protection and a Teensy microcontroller for emergency responses.

Working on creating a version for an 8-cell battery.
