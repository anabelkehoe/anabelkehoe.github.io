Projects

Battery STE

The Battery STE (Specialized Test Equipment) was my primary project, where I designed and developed a system to automate battery charge and discharge cycling for spacecraft battery testing. I designed the system from the initial block diagram through schematic capture and PCB layout in Altium, including high-current switches, voltage and current sensing, CAN communication, fusing, and battery/charger interfaces. I also developed the control software on a Teensy to dynamically adjust the load resistance based on battery voltage, allowing the system to maintain a specified test current. To make the system fully autonomous, I added a Raspberry Pi interface with a web-based user interface for remotely configuring and running tests while logging battery and charger voltage, current, and other test data. I also developed and tested a diode-based solar panel emulator to reproduce the I-V characteristics needed to test the battery’s MPPT charging system; I then created a PCB in Altium for easier production. I also did all of the mechanical enclosure and component sourcing, as well as ordering all PCB’s myself through CircuitHub.

Magnetic Encoder Breakout Board

I designed a breakout board used to measure the angular position of an antenna and interface with its gimbal controller. I reviewed the datasheet for the magnetic sensor IC before implementing necessary features, including differential signaling, a 5 V to 3.3 V LDO, and an onboard temperature sensor. I completed component placement and PCB routing in Altium while maintaining the required board dimensions and required positioning the magnetic sensing IC to interface with the mechanical system. This is a flight board and had to be designed with the conditions of space in mind.

AI&T Battery Charger

I designed the hardware for an AI&T charger board used to charge multiple batteries simultaneously and monitor the voltage output of the charging system. The circuit and PCB were designed to integrate with the power supply and battery, with the board handling the battery charging and voltage monitoring interfaces. I used LTspice to design and evaluate a voltage sense circuit capable of filtering out ground noise. After validating the circuit through simulation, I implemented the design in Altium. This design featured a -3.3V plane, instrumentation amplifier, and I2C MUX.

This project was handed off to me during the last week of my internship. In this time, I was able to create the schematic and PCB, but unfortunately I was not able to conduct a final review of the design and routing, or see the completed board.
